## Diagnosis (why 0.420 open but 0.333 closed, and what is wrong)
1. **Statement found, slot/entity/value not matched.** Same-quoted-statement (evidence-anchored) F1 is ~0.65 for 1.7B r3 vs strict 0.42/0.33. Of 359 gold content ops (open): 146 strict matches, 107 statements untouched (30%), 106 touched but not matched (34 right value/wrong entity, 36 right entity/different value, 36 both differ). Many are naming/granularity differences ("owns the transfers service" vs "role: backend engineer"), some are real errors (dropped or wrong dates).
2. **Closed-loop compounding.** Model memories that do not strictly match a gold slot stay unlinked; by session 4 the store is mostly unlinked rows (mean 5.2 of 8.3 rows). Closed F1 falls by session index (0.402, 0.328, 0.297, 0.277 for sessions 1-4; open stays ~0.37-0.52), gold ops collapse from update 57/confirm 14/retract 17 (open) to 20/4/5 (closed) because slots are "not stored", and duplicates are created.
3. **Over-extraction.** 112 of 337 predicted ops (open) sit on statements with no gold fact; sampling suggests ~25-30% are plausible teacher omissions and the rest are trivial or invented (the guard checks that the quote exists, not that it supports the value). A lexical value-support guard does not help (lowers F1).
4. **Under-extraction by type:** implied facts (recall ~23% vs 43% stated), tasks and plain facts (~32-33%) vs events (65%); statements by other speakers are missed about as often as the user's. `add` precision 0.38 vs `update` 0.76 when used.
5. **Topic boundaries are not a main cause:** in the open loop 85 of 120 sessions show memories from other conversations (about 3 per session), and 1.7B r3 acted on one in only 3 of 76 update/retract/confirm ops (r2: 0); look-alike first-name confusion is about 3% of predicted ops. **`conflict` is untested:** no gold conflict examples exist in train or val.
5b. **Not the main cause:** skip traps (jokes, hypotheticals, hearsay, secrets, injections) account for only ~5% of false memories; hard negatives stored 7%, skip decisions are mostly right (95.8% of recorded skips have the right reason). Retraction is a separate problem: precision 0.32 (open) and 0.11 (closed); confirm recall 0.21.
6. Calendar sessions are easy (F1 0.79); screen (0.32 open, 0.12 closed), email (0.42/0.26), personal and work chats (~0.35) are hard; roman-ur empty sessions are mishandled (empty-session accuracy 0.71 vs 1.0 for English); language slices are too small to rank reliably.
7. Free fix to test: constrained decoding of the contract's enums and the provided memory ids would remove the schema-dropped ops (about 10% of sessions).
